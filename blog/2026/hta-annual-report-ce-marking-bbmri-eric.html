HTA annual report, CE marking and BBMRI-ERIC growth

27 October 2026 · By Pro-curo team · Reviewed by Mark Walker · 5 min read

Why this matters

  • The HTA has begun a multi-year review of its Codes of Practice, which the report says will involve renewing guidance for the sectors it regulates, and is trialling new approaches to managing Corrective and Preventive Action Plans.
  • The government intends to recognise CE marked devices certified under EU MDR and EU IVDR in Great Britain indefinitely, with the existing 30 June 2028 and 30 June 2030 dates in force until the legislation is made.
  • BBMRI-ERIC now spans around 500 biobanks and has agreed to simplify access to samples and data for drug repurposing research, which puts consent and access terms for secondary use in focus.

This fortnight's lead is the Human Tissue Authority's annual report for 2025/26, read against the 2024/25 edition. The government has set out its long-term approach to CE marked devices in Great Britain, and BBMRI-ERIC has added three countries and signed a drug repurposing partnership.

HTA annual report 2025/26: what moved since last year

The HTA's report for 1 April 2025 to 31 March 2026 says it met or exceeded all of its key performance indicators, a first for the organisation, and trialled new approaches to managing Corrective and Preventive Action Plans (CAPAs). It also began a multi-year programme to review and update its Codes of Practice, which the report says will involve renewing guidance for the sectors it regulates.

Last year's report recorded that the HTA met or exceeded almost all of its indicators, with three exceptions: decisions on new licence applications, staff attrition and responsiveness to enquiries and FOI requests. That edition counted 223 inspections of licensed establishments plus 29 linked to licence applications, and highlighted updated incident reporting guidance for the post-mortem sector and new fees published in December 2024.

The 2025/26 report adds several items with practical weight:

  • Evaluated Self-Assessments (ESAs) are now in use in the Public Display sector.
  • Incident reporting (HTARI) was extended to the Anatomy sector, live from 1 December 2025, in response to a recommendation from Sir Jonathan Michael's independent inquiry.
  • The Substances of Human Origin (SoHO) Regulations come into force in Northern Ireland in August 2027, and the HTA reports progress in preparing for them.
  • The HTA licenses over 1,000 premises across six sectors.

We have read the opening sections of both reports. The 2025/26 inspection totals are not in those pages, so the comparison of inspection numbers is not yet made.

What it means for sample custodians: the Codes of Practice review means the standards that research, human application and the other regulated sectors work to will be revised over several years, so teams typically note which SOPs and training materials cite the current codes and watch for HTA consultations. The report says new approaches to managing CAPAs were trialled; the pages we read do not describe them or say whether they will be adopted. Establishments in Northern Ireland have August 2027 as the SoHO date to plan around.

CE marked devices to be recognised in Great Britain indefinitely

The government intends to legislate so that medical devices and in vitro diagnostic (IVD) devices certified under the EU MDR and EU IVDR are recognised in Great Britain indefinitely. It will also align the GB transition for devices certified under the older EU Medical Devices Directive with the EU deadline of 31 December 2028, closing a six-month gap after the current GB cut-off of 30 June 2028.

The consultation ran from 16 February to 10 April 2026 and drew 1,056 responses, with manufacturers the largest group among organisational and professional respondents; 60 per cent supported the transition alignment. A majority also backed indefinite recognition, though some raised concerns about oversight and the UK's ability to independently assure safety and performance, and most opposed a separate reliance route for devices classified higher in GB than in the EU. Manufacturers will still need a UK responsible person, MHRA registration and post-market surveillance, and the approach applies to Great Britain only. The consultation paper estimated that about 90 per cent of devices on the GB market remain CE marked.

What it means for sample custodians: for labs and biobanks using CE marked IVDs and other devices, the stated aim is continuity of supply, but the change still needs legislation, so the existing dates of 30 June 2028 and 30 June 2030, depending on device type, remain the ones in force until it is made. Equipment and procurement records that note each device's marking and the EU legislation it complies with are where teams typically check exposure when the legislation appears.

Portugal joins BBMRI-ERIC, with Montenegro and Ukraine as Observers

BBMRI-ERIC's Assembly of Members approved Portugal as a Member and Montenegro and Ukraine as Observers. The network describes itself as around 500 biobanks within 29 national nodes from 28 Member and Observer countries, plus IARC/WHO.

Portugal's node describes collections covering rare, oncological and neurological diseases, longitudinal population-based cohorts and healthy donors. Montenegro's node emphasises cancer biobanking and standardised biospecimen collection and processing, and Ukraine's coordinated network, the Ukrainian Association of Research Biobanks, was set up in 2021.

What it means for sample custodians: UK teams with partners in these countries now have a national node in each as a point of contact for collections and for shared quality and governance practice. The UK is not on BBMRI-ERIC's published list of Members or Observers.

BBMRI-ERIC and REPO4EU agree to work on drug repurposing

BBMRI-ERIC has signed a Memorandum of Understanding with REPO4EU, a European-global platform for mechanism-based drug repurposing, presented at the RExPO26 conference in Berlin. The agreement covers biomarker discovery, pre-clinical testing, precision pharmacology and medicine, and aims to facilitate and simplify access to samples, data and biomolecular resources across institutions.

REPO4EU's project coordinator, Harald Schmidt, said the collaboration offers a chance to study how predictive and mechanistic biomarkers relate to specific disease phenotypes and how prevalent a biomarker is within a patient population.

What it means for sample custodians: the work described relies on samples with associated data to assess biomarkers and new uses for existing medicines. For custodians in the network, the practical check is whether consent wording and access terms describe that kind of secondary use.

Cell culture contamination that visual checks miss

The Scientist has published a feature on the sources, detection and control of cell culture contamination. Only the feed summary was available to us, and it says monitoring programmes and repositories of tested cells help labs manage contamination that visual checks miss.

What it means for sample custodians: labs that clear cultures mainly by visual inspection can read the full piece against their own testing schedule and the provenance records for cell lines they hold or distribute.

On our radar

  • DHSC and the HTA have published a framework agreement for 2026 to 2029; the page summary says it covers roles and responsibilities, accountability and assurance, governance, and management and financial responsibilities, and we have not yet read the full text.
