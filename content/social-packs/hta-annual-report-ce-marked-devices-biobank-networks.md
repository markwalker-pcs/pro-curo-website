## LinkedIn (company page, Wednesday 10:00 UK)

The Human Tissue Authority met every one of its key performance indicators in 2025/26, a first for the organisation, and ran 226 planned regulatory assessments against 223 the year before.

In the first issue of Sample Matters we read the new annual report against last year's and pick out what changes for people running sample collections:

1. Two-person unannounced inspections continued in the post-mortem sector because of ongoing risk, incidents and shortfalls, while Research inspections moved almost entirely to a single inspector.
2. HTARI incident reporting went live for the Anatomy sector on 1 December 2025, and a multi-year Codes of Practice review is under way.
3. The government intends to recognise CE marked medical and IVD devices in Great Britain indefinitely, with UK responsible person, registration and post-market surveillance duties still in place for manufacturers.

We also cover a new research human milk biobank in Western Australia and Brazil's national biobank network, both reported in the trade press.

Which of these changes will make the biggest difference to how your team prepares for its next inspection?

## Why this matters (for excerpting)

- The HTA ran 226 planned regulatory assessments in 2025/26 and kept two-person unannounced inspections in the post-mortem sector because of ongoing high levels of risk, incidents and shortfalls.
- The HTARI incident reporting system went live for the Anatomy sector on 1 December 2025, and a multi-year Codes of Practice review plus SoHO preparation for Northern Ireland from August 2027 mean guidance that local procedures cite will be revised.
- The government intends to recognise CE marked medical and IVD devices in Great Britain indefinitely, with UK responsible person, MHRA registration and post-market surveillance duties still applying to manufacturers.
